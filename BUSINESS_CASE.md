## The Idea
X ParkWindow is designed for STFX students who commute to campus for class. A student can know exactly when their classes start but not know when to leave home and where to park. Students also need time between finding an open spot that their permit allows and to walk from their car to class. Commuters might solve this issue by leaving extra early, trying lots they're familiarized with, and hoping there's a spot open and trying another lot if it's full. The trade-off between leaving more time than they need and risking late arrival to classes. 

X ParkWindow helps students decide where to park and when to leave before leaving their home. It takes entering their permit type, class location, start and end times, estimated drive time, and walking pace. The planner compares lots and displays an estimated availability window, permit cutoffs, walking time, and suggested departure time. This gives students first choice and backup they can compare ahead of time. 

An annual subscription fee paid by STFX could be a way X ParkWindow generates revenue. The university would receive a planning tool and in a future version with a dashboard showing which parking lots are busiest and at what times. STFX could use the dashboard to see which lots are the busiest and at what times. They can then use that information when deciding whether to add parking. A pilot could test whether students spend less time looking for parking and whether STFX finds the demand high enough to justify paying for the service.  

## Where IT Creates Value
X ParkWindow makes operations more efficient by carrying out a planned and detailed trip in one place. Students enter their permit type, class times, drive duration and walking pace. The app checks which lots they can use dependent on the students permit and when to leave. The app uses the class start time to calculate when to leave, allowing time to drive, park, and walk. In the prototype a student with a daytime permit, a 10am class at Schwartz, and a 10-minute drive gets a recommendation to park in lot P5 and leave at 9:36am. This saves time checking permit rules and figuring out which parking lot to look for a spot in. 

X ParkWindow improves customer satisfaction by showing students what they gain and give up with each parking option. For example, using the same permit, class time, class location and drive time, a user can choose between lot P5 and P7. Users that choose lot P5 has a 61% chance of an open space but a 9-minute walk, whereas users that chooses P7 has a later leave time but only has a 55% chance of an open space and a 4-minute walk to class. Having a backup choice before leaving leaves breathing room to commuters if they are running late but risks the trade-off of possible parking parking spots open. 

## Network Effects 
X ParkWindow creates a one-sided network effect because its main users are STFX student commuters. One-sided network effect is shown when more students use the platform, it becomes more valuable to other students already on it. As more students join the platform, it can collect more information about parking availability, parking patterns and busy hours. Parking predictions would also improve which also help with departure time recommendations. Students would benefit by spending less time searching for a parking spot and diminishing their chances of being late to class. 

Even though X ParkWindow would face a cold-start problem due to the low initial users and limited parking data, STFX could partner with X ParkWindow to promote the platform on the same webpage they sell their parking permits on. This could kickstart the app and have students already logged in before they arrive for the new school year. STFX could also help promote the platform through emails, social media and posters to gain more users. To start, the platform could rely on class schedules, parking regulations, estimated parking patterns and even data collected through a survey answered by students to provide useful recommendations until more users join. 

The parking recommendation page of the platform shows the potential for this network effect by demonstrating estimated parking availabilities, walking times, recommended parking lots and suggested departure times. Even though the current prototype doesn't collect real-time parking information from students, it could be a possible feature in the future. This could strengthen the one-sided network effect because each student who contribute information on the platform would help improve recommendations to other students. Therefore, the platform would become more useful and reliable as increase in student participation occurs. 

## Can It Be Copied
My platform can easily be copied by my classmates using GitHub. A classmate could enter a similar prompt into the AI tool, describe what the website is for, add the same features and how it should work. They could recreate and copy the same parking recommendations, permit selection, class and study locations, walking and departure times. The website and its features do not provide sustainable advantage. Instead, X ParkWindow’s competitive advantage could come from old parking data and partnership with STFX. 

The first resource is old parking data. This resource is valuable because it helps students see when parking lots are busiest and when spaces are most likely to become available. It’s rare because the platform would use parking data collected specifically from STFX, which would make it hard for competitors to have access to. Competitors would need to spend time collecting parking information throughout different class periods, semesters, and special events making it hard to imitate. The platform is not completely non-substitutable because competitors with a bigger budget could install parking sensors or use other methods to collect similar information quicker and more efficiently. 

The second resource is a partnership with STFX parking staff. This partnership would be valuable because the staff could provide updated information about parking regulations, permit changes, and temporary lot closures. If competitors cannot gain access to the same information, it would make the platform rare. Partnership with STFX also means the promotion on the same webpage parking permits are sold at and promotion on the school’s social media. The platform would be hard to imitate because building a friendship, trust, and maintaining a reliable relationship with STFX takes time and effort. Public access to certain information that STFX provides to the public online makes the platform not entirely non-substitutable because competitors could easily access the information just like anyone else.  

A competitor with a bigger budget could easily hire employees, recreate the website and collect parking information and establish a partnership with the university. Even though copying and recreating the website doesn’t take very long, collecting important parking information and data could take a full academic year or longer. X ParkWindow’s competitive advantage could become stronger over time as student users increase and relationship with STFX develops. The platforms long-term success would depend on these two resources improving rather than relying on the website and being the first mover itself.

## Cloud And Operations
Platform as a Service (PaaS) could help the platform by helping run the application, generate departure time, process parking reports, and update the application without managing the server. This could help by lowering costs because there would be no need to buy a physical server, help with maintenance by being in charge of running the platform, and allows for a large amount of users without crashing.

## Risks And Ethics
A risk that X ParkWindow has is student privacy. Student class schedules are used, location and when a student is. Additional to that, students can also put their departure time and length of the drive which then would risk location privacy. To protect their privacy, location sharing can be optional. 

The second risk could be inaccurate parking recommendation. Incorrect predictions could cause students to arrive late to class or not find any spots at all. The app can also be played with and students can lie on it to save their spot by not updating that they have left. To reduce this risk, the platform can show when the information provided was last updated and suggest other parking lots with the most recent updates.  

In the future a sensor connected to the platform could be installed within each parking lots entrances and exits and update the platform in real time. These sensors could show when a car has left and when another has arrived. Although this would only solve the inaccurate parking recommendations, I think it would be a beneficial aspect to the platform to allow for real time data and app updates.
